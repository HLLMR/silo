# Silo on Facebook

> A value-first Facebook plan: a helpful Page for updates plus disciplined, rule-respecting group engagement that answers "which mod crashed me?" and converts goodwill into Discord/site traffic without tripping spam filters or group bans.

## Facebook (groups + page)

### Why Facebook fits Silo

Facebook is where FS25's **largest and most casual** audience actually lives. The tech-savvy minority is on GitHub/Reddit/Discord; the broad middle — the players who run 200+ mods, whose game crashes on load, and who have no idea which mod did it — are in Facebook groups like "Farming Simulator 25 Mods," "FS25 PC Mods," country/language-specific FS groups, and equipment-brand fan groups. These are exactly the people Silo's killer hook was built for: **"names the mod that actually crashed you."**

This audience is also the hardest to win and the easiest to alienate:
- They are burned out on scam mod sites, malware "free mod" links, and self-promoters who drive-by-drop a link and vanish. Groups are heavily moderated and ban link-droppers fast.
- Many are older, non-technical, or non-native-English speakers. A GitHub release page or "Tauri v2 hardlink projection" means nothing to them. They need **plain language, screenshots, and short videos**.
- Trust is everything. Silo's whole pitch (verify the real untampered build, no account, no telemetry, reversible) is *credibility-first* — which is the correct posture for a crowd that's been burned. Lead with the pain relieved, prove it, and let the credibility do the selling.

**Who we reach here:** casual/mid-core FS25 PC players with big, unstable mod folders; troubleshooters who post "game crashes on load, help"; and mod authors who cross-post to groups.

**The voice for THIS platform:** warm, patient, plain-spoken, and genuinely helpful — like a neighbor who's good with computers. **No Gen-Z energy here** (save that for TikTok/Discord). No jargon. Short sentences. Lead with the problem in the player's own words ("Ever had the game crash before the menu even loads?"). Always a real human tone, never corporate. Disclose plainly that you're the developer.

### Account / space setup

**1. Facebook Page (the home base for updates)**
- **Name:** `Silo — FS25 Mod Manager`
- **Username/handle:** `@SiloModManager` (fallbacks: `@GetSilo`, `@SiloForFS25`). Keep it identical in spirit to the site so search and word-of-mouth line up.
- **Category:** Software / Video Game.
- **Profile picture:** the Silo mark on the "Golden Hour" green (`#4a7330`), same icon as the app and site so it's instantly recognizable in a comment thread.
- **Cover image:** a clean screenshot of the crash-attribution feature with a one-line caption overlaid: *"Which mod crashed you? Silo reads your log and tells you."* Use the app's actual palette so Page == product == site.
- **About / Intro:**
  - Short bio: *"Free, open-source mod manager for Farming Simulator 25. Names the mod that crashed you, catches conflicts before launch, and keeps every mod up to date. No account. No telemetry."*
  - Website: `https://silo.hllmr.com`
  - "Additional info" note that it's community-built and open source; link the GitHub and Discord.
- **Buttons/CTA:** primary Page button = "Download" → `silo.hllmr.com`. Add Discord as a secondary link.
- **Pinned post:** the 45–60s "what is Silo" demo video (see launch content) with a plain caption and the download link in the **first comment** (Facebook throttles reach on posts with outbound links in the body — pin the video, drop the link in comment #1, and reference "link in the comments").
- **Featured / Photos highlights:** 4–5 labeled screenshots — (1) crash attribution, (2) conflict warning, (3) one catalog across ModHub/GitHub/Nexus, (4) "verified real build" badge, (5) the undo/reversible view. Each with a one-line human caption.

**2. Personal presence for group engagement**
- Group posting/commenting is done from a **real, transparent personal profile** (the dev's, or a clearly-labeled "Dave @ Silo" identity that is a real person, not a fake persona). Groups distrust posts from Pages; a real human answering a troubleshooting thread is trusted, a Page shilling is not.
- Bio/intro on that profile should make the affiliation obvious ("I build Silo, a free FS25 mod manager"). **Transparency is the guardrail** — never pose as a random happy user. That's astroturfing and it's banned here for a reason.

### Structure (Page layout + group operating model)

**Page tabs / sections (keep it lean):**
- Home (pinned demo video on top)
- About (bio, links, "open source & no telemetry" note)
- Photos/Featured (the 5 labeled feature shots)
- Videos (demo, changelog clips, troubleshooting how-tos)
- Reviews/Recommendations ON (let real users vouch — do **not** solicit fake ones)
- Community/Mentions

**Group engagement model — a tiered target list, not a spray:**
Maintain a living spreadsheet of FS25 Facebook groups with columns: name, member count, language, rules (self-promo allowed? link policy? "no advertising"?), admin contact, and our status (lurking / contributing / cleared-to-share).

- **Tier A — big general FS25 groups (50k+):** value-first only. Answer troubleshooting posts, never lead with a link. Earn the right to mention Silo when it's genuinely the answer.
- **Tier B — troubleshooting / "help my game crashes" groups:** highest-fit. This is where "which mod crashed you" is literally the question being asked daily.
- **Tier C — language/regional groups (DE, FR, PL, ES, etc.):** huge in FS. Only engage where we can post in-language (see the translation skill).
- **Tier D — mod-author & equipment-brand groups:** for the provenance/"verify the real build" angle and author relationships.

**Operating rule of thumb:** for every 1 post that mentions Silo, aim for ~5–7 purely-helpful contributions with no mention. This isn't just etiquette — it's how you avoid the ban that ends the whole channel.

### Content pillars

1. **"Which mod crashed you?" (the hero pillar).** Real before/after: a crash log wall of text → Silo naming the culprit in one line. This is the single most shareable thing we have here.
2. **Troubleshooting help (value-first).** Short how-tos: reading a log, spotting a conflict, why load order matters, safe places to get mods. Silo appears as *a* tool, not the whole point.
3. **Keep-it-current & keep-it-safe.** "Your mod has an update across ModHub/GitHub/Nexus," and the provenance angle — how to know a downloaded mod is the real, untampered build (huge trust topic in this crowd).
4. **Release & changelog news** (translated to plain English — "what this means for you," not raw commit notes).
5. **Community & trust.** Reposting user wins ("Silo found the mod nuking my save — thank you"), answering FAQs, being visibly a real, responsive human. Open-source/no-telemetry reassurance woven in.

### Posting cadence (realistic for a solo/small dev)

- **Page: 3–4 posts/week.** Rough weekly rhythm: 1 hero/feature post, 1 troubleshooting tip, 1 community/user-win or FAQ, and release/changelog posts as they happen. Best windows for this audience: weekday evenings and weekend mornings (US/EU overlap).
- **Groups: daily light-touch presence, ~15–20 min/day.** Answer 2–3 troubleshooting threads with genuine help; mention Silo only where it's the honest best answer and the group's rules allow. Respect each group's self-promo policy (many allow it only on a specific day or in a pinned "promo" thread — use those).
- **Never** cross-post the identical message to many groups the same hour — Facebook flags that as spam and it can restrict the account.

### First-week launch content (concrete)

- **Day 1 — Pinned hero video (Page):** 45–60s screen recording. Hook on screen at 0s: *"Game crashes before the menu even loads? Here's who did it."* Show the log wall-of-text, then Silo naming the mod. End: *"Free. No account. No telemetry. Link in the comments."* Caption in plain language; download link in comment #1.
- **Day 1 — Intro post (Page):** *"Hi, I'm the developer of Silo — a free, open-source mod manager for FS25. I built it because I got tired of my game crashing with 200 mods and no idea which one did it. Happy to answer any questions below."* Honest, human, invites comments (comments = reach).
- **Day 2 — Carousel: "5 things Silo does" (Page):** the 5 labeled screenshots. One line each.
- **Day 2 — First group value posts:** answer 3 real "my game crashes" threads in Tier B groups with actual help; mention Silo only if it fits and rules allow.
- **Day 3 — "How to read your FS25 log" mini-guide (Page + shareable):** genuinely useful even without Silo; Silo shown as the shortcut at the end. This is the post that gets shared into groups *by other people*, which is the goal.
- **Day 4 — Provenance/trust post:** *"How do you know a mod you downloaded is the REAL one and not tampered with? Here's the problem — and how Silo verifies it."* Speaks directly to the malware-scare this crowd knows.
- **Day 5 — "One catalog" post:** short clip showing the same mod's latest version across ModHub, GitHub, and Nexus in one place.
- **Day 6 — FAQ/AMA post:** *"You asked, here are answers"* — is it free (yes), does it touch my save (reversible, nothing destructive), does it phone home (no telemetry), Mac/Linux (experimental).
- **Day 7 — Soft Discord invite:** *"If you want faster help, early builds, and to tell me what to build next, the Discord's here."* Framed as *better support*, not "come boost my server."

**Convert goodwill → Discord/site without getting banned:**
- Put links in **comments and the pinned post**, not stuffed into every group post body.
- Frame the Discord as *where you get help faster / shape the roadmap*, the site as *where you download safely* — a benefit, not an ask.
- Let the shareable how-to posts do the traveling; when others repost them into groups, that's endorsement no amount of self-promo can buy.
- Honor each group's link rules to the letter. One ban in a 100k-member group is a permanent loss of that audience.

### Claude skills to build

Automations a Claude Code agent ("Cowork") builds so a human can run this channel semi-autonomously. Every one of these **drafts for human review and posting** — nothing auto-posts to groups, nothing DMs, nothing mass-cross-posts.

- **`fb-changelog-to-post`**
  - **What:** Turns a raw GitHub release / changelog into a plain-English Facebook Page post ("what this means for you"), stripped of dev jargon, with a hook line, a suggested screenshot/clip slot, and the link pre-placed as comment #1 text.
  - **Trigger:** on new GitHub release (release published webhook / tag push).
  - **Inputs:** release notes markdown, version, repo. **Outputs:** draft post body + first-comment link text + suggested media + recommended post time, dropped into a review queue.

- **`fb-post-scheduler-queue`**
  - **What:** Maintains the week's content calendar across the 5 pillars, balances the mix so it isn't all promo, and queues drafts for human approval (exports to Meta Business Suite scheduler or a simple approve-then-post step). Enforces cadence caps and the no-identical-cross-post rule.
  - **Trigger:** weekly (e.g. Sunday) + on-demand.
  - **Inputs:** pillar templates, backlog of drafts, prior post history. **Outputs:** a scheduled/queued week of drafts, each flagged pillar + target (Page vs. which group).

- **`fb-troubleshooting-triage`**
  - **What:** Reviews screenshots/text of crash logs a user pasted (from a monitored inbox/DM the dev forwards in), identifies the likely culprit mod and gives a plain-English fix, and drafts a **helpful, non-salesy** reply the human can post. Suggests whether a Silo mention is appropriate for that thread's group rules.
  - **Trigger:** on new forwarded troubleshooting item / manual.
  - **Inputs:** log text or screenshot, group context + rules. **Outputs:** diagnosis + draft reply (with/without Silo mention flagged), plus a "reusable FAQ?" tag.

- **`fb-clip-cutter`**
  - **What:** From a screen recording of the app, cuts a 30–60s vertical/square clip with an on-screen hook caption and captions burned in (many watch muted), sized for Page video + Reels.
  - **Trigger:** on new raw recording dropped in a watch folder / on new feature demo.
  - **Inputs:** raw mp4 + the feature name + hook line. **Outputs:** trimmed captioned clip(s) + 3 caption/hook variants.

- **`fb-screenshot-annotator`**
  - **What:** Takes app screenshots and produces the labeled, on-brand feature images (Golden Hour palette, one-line caption overlay) for the Photos/Featured section and carousels.
  - **Trigger:** on-demand / on new feature.
  - **Inputs:** raw screenshots + captions. **Outputs:** annotated, correctly-sized images.

- **`fb-group-rules-tracker`**
  - **What:** Maintains the tiered group spreadsheet — records each group's self-promo/link rules, promo-day windows, and our engagement status; warns before a mention would violate a rule and reminds when a "promo day" opens.
  - **Trigger:** weekly refresh + on adding a new group.
  - **Inputs:** group list, pasted rules text. **Outputs:** updated rules matrix + a "safe-to-mention-today" list.

- **`fb-comment-triage`**
  - **What:** Summarizes new comments/mentions across the Page, flags questions needing a human reply, drafts suggested responses in the warm plain-spoken voice, and surfaces recurring questions as FAQ-post candidates. Flags anything hostile for human-only handling.
  - **Trigger:** daily.
  - **Inputs:** comment/mention export. **Outputs:** prioritized reply list + draft responses + FAQ candidates.

- **`fb-post-translator`**
  - **What:** Localizes an approved Page post or troubleshooting reply into DE/FR/PL/ES for regional groups, keeping the plain, human tone (not literal machine-ese).
  - **Trigger:** on-demand when targeting a Tier C group.
  - **Inputs:** approved English draft + target language. **Outputs:** localized draft flagged for a native-speaker sanity check.

- **`fb-user-win-collector`**
  - **What:** Watches for organic "Silo found the mod that crashed me / thank you" comments and posts, drafts a permission-to-repost ask, and queues an approved testimonial post. Never fabricates or solicits fake praise.
  - **Trigger:** daily scan.
  - **Inputs:** mentions/comments feed. **Outputs:** candidate testimonials + permission-ask draft + queued repost.

### Metrics that matter here

- **Referral traffic** from Facebook to `silo.hllmr.com` (UTM-tagged links) → downloads. This is the real scoreboard.
- **Discord joins attributed to FB** (invite link with a distinct vanity/UTM).
- **Shares and saves** (not just likes) — the how-to posts getting reshared *into* groups by others is the strongest signal.
- **Comment quality/volume** — questions answered, sentiment; comments drive reach far more than likes.
- **Group goodwill health:** number of troubleshooting threads helped, ratio of help-posts to promo-posts, and zero rule-strikes/bans.
- Page follows and video watch-through are secondary vanity metrics — track, don't optimize for.

### Guardrails

- **No astroturfing, ever.** Group engagement is from a real, disclosed identity. No fake "happy user" accounts, no sock puppets, no engagement-buying, no vote/comment manipulation.
- **Respect every group's rules** — especially self-promo/link policies and promo-day-only windows. Where in doubt, ask an admin. One ban costs an entire audience.
- **No mass-DMing, no auto-posting to groups, no identical cross-posts in bulk.** Automations draft and schedule; a human posts and engages.
- **Disclose the dev relationship** and the AI-assisted nature of drafted content where norms expect it. Facebook + Meta ToS compliance throughout.
- **Honesty in claims:** "no telemetry," "reversible," "verifies the real build" must stay literally true in every post. This audience punishes overpromising harder than any other.

### Effort / priority

- **Priority: HIGH.** This is the biggest, best-fit, and most underserved Silo audience — but also the slowest-trust and highest-ban-risk, so it rewards patience over blitz.
- **Setup effort (week 0):** ~1 day — Page, handle, cover, pinned video, 5 featured shots, group spreadsheet seeded.
- **Ongoing effort:** ~30–45 min/day (mostly group troubleshooting + comment replies) + ~2 hrs/week for Page content, once the Claude skills are drafting for you. The skills cut the content-production time by well over half; the human's job becomes *approve, personalize, and be genuinely helpful in threads* — which is the part that can't and shouldn't be automated.
- **Build order for the skills:** `fb-changelog-to-post` and `fb-clip-cutter`/`fb-screenshot-annotator` first (they unblock launch-week content), then `fb-troubleshooting-triage` and `fb-group-rules-tracker` (they make daily group work safe and fast), then `fb-comment-triage`, `fb-post-translator`, and `fb-user-win-collector`.
